# European Accessibility Act — context

Short, practical background so the audit can flag readiness. This is not legal advice; confirm scope with a qualified source for a specific business.

## What it is

The European Accessibility Act (EAA), Directive (EU) 2019/882, sets common EU accessibility requirements for certain products and services sold to consumers. Each member state has transposed it into national law (in the Netherlands, via national implementing legislation).

## When

The requirements apply from **28 June 2025**. Some products already on the market or existing service contracts may have limited transition periods, but new products and services offered from that date are expected to conform.

## Who and what is in scope

It targets products and services including:

- e-commerce (online shops and their checkout),
- consumer banking and payment services,
- electronic communications and their websites/apps,
- transport service websites, apps, and ticketing,
- e-books and e-readers,
- computers, operating systems, smartphones, TV equipment, ATMs, and ticket/check-in machines.

Ordinary informational or brochure websites are not automatically in scope, but the moment a site sells to consumers, its e-commerce flow generally is.

## The exemption most small businesses ask about

**Microenterprises that provide services** are exempted from the service obligations. A microenterprise employs fewer than 10 people **and** has an annual turnover or balance sheet total not exceeding €2 million. Note this service exemption does not apply in the same way to microenterprises that manufacture, import, or distribute in-scope products, and it does not remove the general good practice of being accessible.

## The technical standard

Conformance is assessed against the harmonised standard **EN 301 549**, which for web content points to **WCAG (2.1) level AA**. Auditing to **WCAG 2.2 AA** (this skill's default) meets and slightly exceeds that bar, since 2.2 is backwards compatible with 2.1.

## What this means for the audit

- If the target is or contains an in-scope service (especially e-commerce checkout, banking, ticketing), treat AA failures as compliance risks, not just quality issues, and say so.
- If the business is likely a microenterprise providing a service, note the possible exemption but still report the issues: the exemption is a legal shield, not a reason to exclude disabled customers.
- Always frame the readiness note as "areas of risk to review", not "you are compliant / non-compliant". Certification is a separate signed process.
